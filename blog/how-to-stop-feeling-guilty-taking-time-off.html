How to Stop Feeling Guilty About Taking Time Off

Direct answer

The guilt about taking time off is usually a priorities signal. If your heart is genuinely with God and your family first, and the business sits below them, then time with the people you love is not stolen from work, it is the point of the work. Check your priorities honestly and often, align your will with God's, and rest with them stops feeling like guilt.

Guilt about time off is a priorities signal

When taking time off nags at you, do not just push the feeling down. Read it. That guilt is often telling you the business has quietly climbed higher on your list than it should be. So check yourself, on a weekly basis, maybe even daily, as often as you can, and ask honestly where your priorities actually lie right now, not where you say they are.

Guilt can mean there is something to bring to Jesus Christ

Here is another layer to it. Guilt often means there is probably something to repent for, some place where your heart or your priorities have drifted. So do not just work it out in your own head. Bring it to Jesus Christ. Ask Him for help and for guidance, and then actually listen. You will know. He will show you what needs to change, and He will give you the strength to change it.

Would you give the business away if you had to?

Here is the test I come back to. You should be willing to give away the whole business if it came to that, because you need to know where your heart really is. Then run your desires through a litmus test. Is your desire for the business bigger than it ought to be? There is nothing wrong with wanting to build and win. But when the wanting starts overriding God and your family, that is the thing to catch, and it is exactly what the guilt is pointing at.

Align your will with God's

As a Christian man, you are on a covenant path to become even as Jesus Christ is, and that calls for constant self-evaluation. Is your will aligned with what the Lord would have for you? Does His will actually mean more to you than anything else? Are you willing to let His will prevail against any other voice you hear, including the one in your own head telling you to keep grinding? At the end of the day, that is what matters most.

When your priorities are right, rest is not guilt

Here is the freedom in it. When God and your family are truly first, and you have honestly put them there, time with them is not a distraction from your purpose. It is your purpose. The guilt loosens its grip because you are no longer stealing from something more important, you are giving to it. And as you order your life that way, the blessings are yours, even life eternal.

Frequently asked questions

Why do I feel guilty taking time off as a business owner?

Guilt about time off is usually a priorities signal. If it nags at you, it often means the business has quietly climbed higher up your list than it should be. When you honestly check your heart and put God and family first, time with them stops registering as time stolen from work.

How do I stop feeling guilty for resting?

Check your priorities honestly and often, even weekly or daily. Ask whether your desire for the business has grown bigger than it should be, and get your will aligned with God's. When God and your family are genuinely first, rest with them is not something to feel guilty about. It is exactly what you are supposed to be doing.

Is it wrong for a Christian to take time off from work?

No. The question is where your heart is. A Christian man is called to keep God first and to love and be present for his family. When those priorities are actually in order, taking time for them is not a distraction from your purpose, it is part of it, and the blessings follow.
