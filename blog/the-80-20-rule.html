The 80/20 Rule: How to Know What to Actually Focus On

Direct answer

The 80/20 rule, also called the Pareto principle, says roughly 80% of your results come from about 20% of your effort. To know what to focus on, find that 20%, protect it on your calendar first, and cut or delegate the busywork that only feels productive.

What the 80/20 rule actually is

The Pareto principle is a simple observation that holds up almost everywhere: results are never evenly distributed. A small share of what you do produces most of what you get. Roughly 20% of your effort drives about 80% of your results. The exact numbers shift, but the pattern doesn't. A few things always matter far more than the rest.

Success leaves clues

You don't have to guess at your 20%. Success leaves clues. Look at what has already produced your best results: which clients, which offers, which specific actions keep showing up right before the wins. Those repeated patterns are pointing straight at the small set of things that actually move your number.

How do you find your 20%?

Two lists. First, write down the handful of actions that have driven your biggest results. Second, look honestly at where your time actually went this week. Then compare them. Most people are shocked at how little of their week is spent on the first list, and how much is spent on things that felt busy but changed nothing.

Protect the 20%, cut the 80%

Finding your 20% does nothing if it doesn't get protected. Put it on the calendar first, before the busywork fills the space. Then cut, delegate, or automate as much of the low-value 80% as you can. This is where the time management piece comes in, and it's why the fix is rarely a longer to-do list.

Why this beats just working more hours

More hours on the wrong 80% still produces very little. That's the trap most driven people fall into: they try to out-work a focus problem. The 80/20 rule is how you produce more without simply grinding more, by pouring your best energy into the few things that were always going to matter most. When the real thing blocking you is avoidance instead of focus, that's usually procrastination, and it has its own fix.

Frequently asked questions

What is the 80/20 rule?

The 80/20 rule, also called the Pareto principle, is the observation that roughly 80% of your results come from about 20% of your effort. A small number of actions drive most of the outcome, and the rest is mostly busywork that only feels productive.

How do I find my 20%?

Success leaves clues. Look at what has already produced your best results: which clients, which offers, which actions. Those repeated wins point straight at your 20%. Then look at where your time actually goes and notice how much of it isn't on that list.

How is the 80/20 rule used in business?

You find the small set of activities driving most of your revenue or growth, protect that time on the calendar first, and cut, delegate, or automate as much of the low-value 80% as you can. It's how you produce more without simply working more hours.

Is the 80/20 rule always exactly 80/20?

No. The numbers are a pattern, not a law. Sometimes it's 90/10 or 70/30. The point isn't the exact split. It's that results are never evenly distributed, so a few things always matter far more than the rest.
