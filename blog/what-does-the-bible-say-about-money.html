What Does the Bible Say About Money?

Direct answer

The Bible does not call money evil. It calls the love of money a root of evil. Money itself is a tool, and Scripture treats it as something to steward, earn honestly, hold loosely, and use to provide for your family, serve others, and build God's kingdom. The danger is never the money. It is letting money become the master you serve instead of God.

Money is a tool, not a master

Jesus Christ said it plainly: no one can serve two masters, you cannot serve both God and money. Notice the word, serve. Money makes a wonderful servant and a brutal master. For an entrepreneur that is the whole test. You can use money to provide, to give, and to build something good, or you can spend your life serving it while it quietly costs you your faith, your family, and your peace. The question is never how much you have. It is who is serving whom.

The love of money, not money, is the root of evil

This is the most misquoted verse about money in the Bible. Scripture does not say money is the root of all evil. It says the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil. That is a different thing entirely. Plenty of faithful people in Scripture were wealthy, from Abraham to Job to Solomon, and God was not against their wealth. The danger lives in the heart, when the pursuit of money becomes the thing you love and chase above God and the people He gave you.

You are called to provide

On the other side, Scripture does not romanticize being broke. It says that anyone who does not provide for his own household has denied the faith. Making money to take care of your wife, your kids, and the people depending on you is not unspiritual. It is a responsibility God actually hands you. Earning well, charging fairly for honest work, and building something that provides is part of living your faith out, not a departure from it. Faith first, then family, then the business that supports them, in that order.

Hold it loosely, and give

The clearest sign that money is a tool and not your master is how freely you give it away. God loves a cheerful giver, one who gives from the heart and not under pressure. Money held with an open hand blesses the people around you and keeps your own heart free. An entrepreneur who makes more only to grip it tighter has missed the point. The income is meant to flow to your family, your people, and His kingdom, not just to pile up.

Contentment is the antidote to greed

Scripture pairs money with a warning and a cure. The warning is the love of money. The cure is contentment: keep your life free from the love of money, and be content with what you have. The chase for more, with no contentment underneath it, never ends, there is always a next number. Contentment does not mean you stop growing or building. It means your peace is not for sale, and your worth was never tied to your bank account in the first place.

Multiply what you have been given

None of this means you bury your money to keep it safe. In the parable of the talents, the servant Jesus praised was the one who put what he was given to work and multiplied it, and the one He rebuked was the one who buried it out of fear. Money, like every other talent God hands you, is meant to be stewarded and grown for good, not hidden. The faithful move is to take what you have, multiply it through honest work, and point it at the people and the mission that matter.

Frequently asked questions

Is money evil according to the Bible?

No. The Bible says the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil, not money itself. Money is a tool, and Scripture is full of faithful people who had wealth. The caution is always on the heart behind it, not the dollars.

What does the Bible say about making money and wealth?

It does not condemn it. You are actually told to provide for your household, and charging fairly for honest work is part of serving people well. Wealth becomes a problem only when you love it or serve it instead of God, or let the chase cost your faith and family.

Can you serve God and money at the same time?

No. Jesus Christ said no one can serve two masters, God and money. You can use money to serve God, provide, and give, but you cannot serve both. The entrepreneur's job is to keep money a tool and never the master.

What does the Bible say about giving and generosity?

God loves a cheerful giver, one who gives from the heart rather than under pressure. Holding money loosely and giving it freely is one of its best uses, and it keeps your own heart free from the grip of greed.
